Frequently Asked Questions about the Life In The UK test

What is the format of the test and what is the pass mark?

The Life in the UK test is a multiple choice test taken on a computer at the test centre:

  • 24 questions
  • 45 minutes to answer them
  • You need 75% to pass, which is 18 correct answers out of 24

The questions are drawn from the official handbook, Life in the UK: A Guide for New Residents, and cover British history, traditions, values, law and government. You can practise with our free practice tests, which use the same format and timing.

How can I book the Life in the UK test?

You can book the Life in the UK test online through the official Life in the UK Test website. Follow the instructions on the website to select a test centre, date, and time that suits you. Make sure to read all instructions carefully and enter your details correctly when booking.

How much does the Life in the UK test cost?

The Life in the UK test costs £50. This fee is non-refundable, so make sure you are fully prepared before booking your test.

What happens if I fail? Can I retake the test?

Yes. There is no limit on the number of times you can take the Life in the UK test, but two rules apply:

  • You must wait at least 7 days between attempts.
  • You pay the £50 fee again for every attempt.

You are not told which questions you got wrong, so the best preparation for a retake is to work through the chapters again and use the practice tests until you score comfortably above the pass mark.

How long is the Life in the UK test valid?

The Life in the UK test certificate does not expire. Once you pass the test, your certificate remains valid indefinitely.

Where can I take the Life in the UK test?

You can take the Life in the UK test at one of the approved test centres across the UK. When booking your test, you will be able to choose a test centre that is convenient for you. Visit the official Life in the UK Test website to find a test centre near you.

What should I bring on the day of the test?

Bring the same ID document you used when booking, and make sure it is valid and not expired. Accepted documents include a passport, a biometric residence permit, a UK photocard driving licence, or a travel document. You must also bring proof of your postcode, such as a recent utility bill or bank statement.

A few practical points:

  • Arrive early. If you are late you may not be allowed to sit the test.
  • If your name or address does not match your booking, you can be turned away — and the £50 fee is not refunded.
  • Phones and personal belongings are stored away during the test.
  • You get a short practice run of sample questions before the real test starts, so you can get used to the screen.

Check the official guidance for the current list of accepted documents before you travel.

Who needs to take the Life in the UK test?

The Life in the UK test is required for individuals applying for:

  • British citizenship
  • Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR)

Some exemptions apply, such as for individuals under 18 or over 65 years of age, or those with certain medical conditions. Check the official guidance for more details.
